# v0.2.4 density-contrast benchmark

## Scope

`benchmarks/protocol.v0.2.4-density-contrast.json` is a separate,
noncanonical stress test. It does not replace, append to, or change any frozen
v0.2.4 evidence. It runs the unmodified v0.2.4 StrataSCAN defaults and the
existing controlled baseline profiles under `target-discovery-v1` evaluation.
All methods receive the same three fresh generated-data seeds (211, 223, 227),
so comparisons are paired by realization and never tune a method from labels.

The family targets StrataSCAN's central assumption directly: its fixed shell
ranks observe a distribution of local core-distance events, and its adaptive
Gamma stratification is intended to separate density regimes before
connectivity extraction. A method using one global distance scale should find
the high-contrast end increasingly difficult. This is a stress-test
hypothesis, not an assumed result.

## Controlled design

Every case contains 10,000 rows, six target clusters, 1,250 rows per target,
and 25% common uniform background. Target centers lie on a radius-8 circle, so
adjacent centers are exactly 8 units apart. The background support is
`[-12, 12]^2` in the separating coordinates and `[-3, 3]` in any additional
coordinates. Counts, centers, separation, background fraction, and background
support do not change with density ratio or shape.

For dimension `d`, the six requested relative peak densities are geometrically
spaced from `R` to 1. If target `i` has count `m_i` and requested relative peak
density `q_i`, its isotropic standard deviation is

```text
s_i = 0.75 * ((m_i / m_sparsest) / q_i) ** (1 / d).
```

Thus the Gaussian peak-mass proxy
`m_i / ((2*pi) ** (d/2) * sqrt(det(cov_i)))` has a declared densest-to-sparsest
ratio of exactly `R`. Protocol counts divide evenly, giving the simpler radius
ratio `R ** (1/d)`. The anisotropic variant applies a rotated determinant-one
axis transform with standard-deviation multipliers 2 and 0.5: the longest axis
is four times the shortest while peak density is unchanged.

The matrix contains:

- dimensions 2 and 8;
- declared peak-density ratios 16, 64, and 256 for isotropic Gaussians;
- additional rotated-anisotropic cases at ratios 64 and 256 in 2D and 256 in
  8D;
- 9 cases x 9 methods x 3 paired seeds = 243 jobs.

At ratio 256, the analytic densest-to-sparsest local distance-scale ratio is
16 in 2D and 2 in 8D. The latter intentionally checks the dimensional effect:
the determinant-defined density contrast is equally strong while marginal
radius differences are smaller.

## Permitted claim

The experiment may support a family- and geometry-scoped statement such as:

> Under six separated equal-mass Gaussian targets with 25% uniform background,
> method A retained target recovery as the declared peak-density ratio increased
> from 16 to 256 in the tested 2D/8D and isotropic/anisotropic cases.

It cannot establish global clustering superiority, performance on touching or
non-Gaussian biological populations, or robustness to changing class balance,
separation, or background construction. Results must report failures in the
completion denominator and stratify by dimension, shape, and density ratio.

## Execution

Run the complete matrix from an environment exposing this checkout's `src/`:

```powershell
python -m benchmarks.run_benchmark `
  --protocol benchmarks/protocol.v0.2.4-density-contrast.json `
  --suite synthetic `
  --output-dir results/runs/v0.2.4-density-contrast `
  --max-workers 1
```

Resume only with the identical command plus `--resume`. The default evaluator
is `benchmarks/evaluation_protocol.v1.json`; keep it explicit in any archived
command record. A smoke result is diagnostic only and must not be merged into
the 243-job evidence matrix.

Every target met the locked purity/coverage discovery rule in every seed and
case. Median target F1 ranged from 0.829 to 0.929. The unfavorable endpoint is

that complete thresholded discovery does not imply uniformly high recovery.
Candidate burden also remains material: the median execution produced 18
clusters for six matched targets (12 unmatched), with 8--40 predicted and
2--34 unmatched clusters across cells.
The defensible result is therefore that current defaults recover all declared
targets across the 16--256 peak-density contrast matrix, with a visible
coverage and fragmentation cost on anisotropic and 8D cases.
